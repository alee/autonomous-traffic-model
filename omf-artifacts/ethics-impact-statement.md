# Ethics impact statement
Version: 0.1 | Date: 2026-11-17 | Status: draft, unreviewed | Authority: OMFA | Author: Codex with user-supplied intent

## Intended Use and Decision Authority
Exploratory research comparing conditional outcomes. No operational vehicle control, deployment certification, liability attribution, or enforcement decisions are supported. People choose acceptable tradeoffs; simulation exposes consequences.

## Value Assumptions
Keep safety outcomes, person and vehicle delay, reliability, throughput, and access distributions separate. No single weighted welfare score has been selected. A Pareto frontier can include socially unacceptable points: acceptability thresholds remain a human decision.

## Representational Harms
Do not equate independent ownership, human driving, loss of connectivity, or equipment age with maliciousness. Do not assign intent from neighborhood identity. Group results by operationally relevant cohorts and geographic burden without implying inferred individual intent. Vehicle-only metrics can hide transit and walking burdens.

## Vulnerable Populations
Explicitly track transit-dependent riders, disabled travelers, pedestrians, cyclists, emergency patients, and residents on diversion routes. Crossing and accessibility demands require representation; their absence limits any citywide welfare conclusion.

## Misuse Risks
An ideal-information reference could be mistaken for deployable capability. Simulated robustness could be misrepresented as real-world safety assurance. Automatic exclusion of suspected actors could punish benign failures or induce gridlock.

## Mitigation Strategies
Use bounded simulation scenarios, synthetic identities, transparent actor classification rules, distributional metrics, and explicit information constraints. Report false suspicion and burden from conservative fallback if detection is modeled. Require participatory review and independent ethics review before any consequential allocation or real-world policy use.

## Transparency and Accountability
All results remain conditional on represented populations, dynamics, and scenarios. City and operator authority are unresolved; no agency endorsement exists. Public communication must distinguish results, interpretation, speculation, and normative preferences.
